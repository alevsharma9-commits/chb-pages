## 1. Property tax calendar

- Texas has no state property tax. Local governments set rates and collect property taxes. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/basics.php)
- Appraisal districts appraise property value as of Jan. 1 each year. Market conditions and who owns the property on that date determine whether it is taxable, the value, and who owes the tax. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/basics.php)
- Jan. 1: date taxable values and qualification for certain exemptions are determined, and the date a tax lien attaches to property to secure taxes, penalties and interest for the year (Tax Code 11.42(a), 23.01(a), 32.01(a)). (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php)
- Jan. 2: rendition period begins. April 15: last day to file renditions on most property types; chief appraiser must extend to May 15 on written request. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php)
- April 1 (or as soon as practicable after): chief appraiser mails notices of appraised value for single-family residence homesteads. May 1 (or as soon as practicable after): notices for other properties (Sec. 25.19(a)). (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php)
- April 30: last day to file some exemption applications and certain special-appraisal applications with the appraisal district. General exemption filing deadline is before May 1. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php; https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/)
- May 15: "Last day to file most protests with ARB (or by 30th day after notice of appraised value is delivered, whichever is later)" (Sec. 41.44(a)(1)). Around May 15 the appraisal review board (ARB) begins hearing protests. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php; https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/basics.php)
- July 20: ARB must approve appraisal records (may not if more than 5 percent of total appraised value remains under protest). July 25: chief appraiser certifies appraisal roll to each taxing unit. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php)
- Sept. 29: last day for taxing units to adopt the tax rate (or before the 60th day after receiving the certified roll, whichever is later). (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php)
- Oct. 1: tax assessor mails tax bills (or soon after as practical). Bills go out in October and payment is due upon receipt. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php; https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/pay)
- Split payment: first half due on or before Nov. 30; second half last day June 30 of the next year (Sec. 31.03(a)). (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php)
- Payment deadline: "In most cases, you must pay your property taxes by Jan. 31. Taxes that remain unpaid on Feb. 1 are considered delinquent." Units must give at least 21 days after mailing the bill. If the bill is mailed after Jan. 10, the delinquency date moves to the first day of the next month giving at least 21 days (example printed: bill mailed Jan. 15, delinquent March 1). (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/pay)
- Penalty and interest: on Feb. 1 the collector adds a six percent penalty and one percent interest. Penalty continues at one percent per month until July 1, when the penalty becomes 12 percent. Interest is one percent per month with no maximum. Private attorneys hired by units can charge an additional penalty of up to 20 percent. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/pay)
- If a taxing unit with an attorney contract provides for it, the attorney-fee penalty applies to taxes delinquent Feb. 1 to May 1 that remain delinquent on July 1 (Sec. 33.07). (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php; https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/bills/misc-bills.php)
- Over-65 or disabled homeowners (and disabled veterans) may pay in four installments: first by Feb. 1, then March 31 / May 31 / July 31 deadlines on the calendar (Sec. 31.031); installment penalty is six percent of the delinquent installment amount, interest one percent per month. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php; https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/bills/misc-bills.php)
- Waiver of penalty and interest (Sec. 33.011): in most instances the tax must be paid by the 21st day after the owner knows of the delinquency and the waiver requested in writing before the 181st day after the delinquency date. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/pay)
- Installment plans on delinquent taxes: some collectors allow up to 36 months; required only on a residence homestead. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/pay)
- Appeals after the ARB: district court, regular binding arbitration, or State Office of Administrative Hearings (SOAH) when the ARB value is over $1,000,000. Owners appealing must pay taxes (usually the undisputed amount) before the delinquency date. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/basics.php; https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/pay)
- Residence homestead appraisal cap (Sec. 23.23): appraised value may not increase more than 10% per year (lesser of market value or 10% over prior-year appraised value plus new improvements), effective Jan. 1 of the year after the homestead exemption is first qualified. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/appraisal-tax-limitations.php)

## 2. Homestead exemption, over-65/disabled freeze, disabled veterans

- School districts must give a $140,000 residence homestead exemption (Sec. 11.13(b)). Any taxing unit may adopt a local option exemption of up to 20 percent of appraised value, not less than $5,000 (Sec. 11.13(n)). Counties with farm-to-market or flood control taxes must give $3,000 (Sec. 11.13(a)). (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/)
- Owner must have an ownership interest and use the home as principal residence, and may not claim another residence homestead in or outside Texas. A buyer acquiring after Jan. 1 may get the exemption for the rest of that tax year immediately if the prior owner did not receive it. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/)
- Age 65 or older or disabled: school districts must give an additional $60,000 residence homestead exemption (Sec. 11.13(c)). Local option exemption (Sec. 11.13(d)) cannot be less than $3,000. A disabled person must meet the Social Security disability-benefit definition. A surviving spouse age 55 or older may keep the deceased spouse's 65-or-older exemption in certain cases. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/)
- Tax ceiling (freeze), Sec. 11.26: a school district may not increase the total annual tax on the residence homestead of an owner age 65 or older or disabled; the limit is generally the school tax imposed in the year the owner first qualified. Surviving spouse age 55 or older may continue. Ceiling can transfer proportionately to a new Texas homestead. New improvements (other than repairs or ordinary maintenance) can raise it. Counties, cities and junior college districts may adopt a similar limit under Sec. 11.261. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/appraisal-tax-limitations.php)
- Disabled veteran exemption, Sec. 11.22, applies to any one property the veteran owns. Table as printed: 10% to 29% rating, up to $5,000; 30% to 49%, up to $7,500; 50% to 69%, up to $10,000; 70% to 100%, up to $12,000. A veteran age 65 or older with a rating of at least 10 percent, totally blind in one or both eyes, or who lost use of a limb may get $12,000. Surviving spouse (unmarried) and children under 18 may qualify. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/disabledvet-faq.php)
- 100 percent disabled veteran (100% VA compensation or individual unemployability): total exemption on the residence homestead under Sec. 11.131; extends to an unmarried surviving spouse under stated conditions. Sec. 11.132 covers a homestead donated by a charity (percentage equals disability rating); Sec. 11.133 total exemption for surviving spouse of a service member killed in the line of duty. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/; https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/disabledvet-faq.php)
- Disabled veteran application deadline is April 30, but Sec. 11.22 applicants have up to five years after the delinquency date to apply; surviving spouse of a veteran with a donated homestead has two years. Forms: 50-135 (Sec. 11.22), 50-114 (homestead exemptions). (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/disabledvet-faq.php)
- Inherited (heir) property homestead: heirs not named on a recorded deed must give the appraisal district an affidavit of ownership interest, the prior owner's death certificate, a recent utility bill, and any court record citation. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/)

## 3. Transfer tax, deed recording, recording fees

- NOT OPENED: statement that Texas has no state transfer tax. Nothing opened mentions a transfer or documentary tax on deeds; the Comptroller property-tax and county clerk pages describe only recording fees.
- County clerk is county recorder. Deed records include general warranty deed, special warranty deed, quitclaim deed, trustee's deed, sheriff's deed, tax deed, affidavits, deeds of trust releases, leases, contracts of sale, powers of attorney. Deed records are filed in the county where the real property is located. (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)
- An instrument conveying real property can be recorded only if signed and acknowledged or sworn before two subscribing witnesses or before an officer authorized to take acknowledgments (notary, county clerk, district clerk, county court judge) (Prop. Code 12.001(b)). (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)
- Recording fee structure (2023 manual): first page $5.00; each additional page or part of page or attachment $4.00; each name over five to be indexed $0.25 (Loc. Gov't Code 118.012, 118.013). A missing grantee mailing address may add $25. (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)
- Additional clerk fees listed: records management and preservation fee not more than $10; records archive fee not more than $10; records technology and infrastructure fee $2.00 (Loc. Gov't Code 118.011). (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)
- Commissioners court may direct the clerk to waive real property recording fees for buyers using certain federal or state grant programs (118.0135). (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)

## 4. Nonjudicial foreclosure

- Three types: judicial (rare in Texas), non-judicial (power of sale in a deed of trust; Prop. Code 51.002), expedited (court order, required for judicial route on home equity loans, reverse mortgages, owners' association assessments). (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/foreclosure)
- In most home loans, federal rule bars starting foreclosure until the loan is over 120 days delinquent. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code 16.035 sets a 4-year limitations period on foreclosure actions with exceptions. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/foreclosure/before-the-sale)
- Notice of default; borrower may reinstate by paying the past-due amount within 20 days of receiving the notice (51.002(d)). After the 20-day period and at least 21 days before the sale, the servicer sends the Notice of Sale; it must also be posted on the courthouse door and filed with the county clerk. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/foreclosure/before-the-sale)
- Sale must be a public auction between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. on the first Tuesday of a month, or the first Wednesday if the first Tuesday is Jan. 1 or July 4 (AG informal guidance). Sale starts at the notice time, no later than three hours after. (https://texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/informal-guidance-concerning-foreclosure-sales; https://guides.sll.texas.gov/foreclosure/the-sale)
- County clerk manual: sales made in the county where the real estate sits; notice at least 21 days before the sale posted at the courthouse door and filed with the clerk (clerk fee $2.00 per notice); commissioners court designates the sale area, recorded in real property records; notice must carry the active-duty military statement. (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)
- After the sale: deficiency suit must be filed within two years of the sale; borrower may request fair market value offset. Excess funds go to junior liens, then former owner. Redemption right exists only for specific kinds of foreclosure such as certain tax liens and owners' association assessment liens. A buyer facing an occupant may need an eviction case. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/foreclosure/after-the-sale; https://txcourts.gov/media/916488/130337d.pdf)

## 5. Eviction

- Notice to vacate (Prop. Code 24.005): at least 3 days' written notice unless the lease says otherwise; for nonpayment where tenant was on time the prior month, notice to pay rent or vacate. Delivery: in person to tenant or household member 16 or older, inside the premises in an obvious place, by mail, or e-mail if the written lease allows. 30-day federal CARES notice may apply to covered properties. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/eviction-process)
- Suit filed in justice court. Tenant served at least 4 days before trial. Written answer not required (except summary disposition); hearing set no sooner than 10 days and no later than 21 days after filing. Jury demand at least 3 days before trial. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/eviction-process)
- Appeal to county court must be filed within 5 days of judgment (bond, cash deposit, or statement of inability to afford costs). (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/eviction-process; https://txcourts.gov/media/1461822/259105.pdf)
- Writ of possession: available 6 days after final judgment; officer serves it within 5 days; 24-hour notice before removal; cannot issue more than 60 days after judgment (court may allow 90 days for good cause); cannot be executed after the 90th day. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/eviction-process)
- Rule 510 does not govern pre-suit notices; those are in Property Code ch. 24. Rent claim may be joined if not more than $20,000 (text from a Dec 2025 order with proposed amendments, check current rule). (https://txcourts.gov/media/1461822/259105.pdf)

## 6. Probate, small estate affidavit, estate tax

- Will probate window: 4 years from death; court may accept later for acceptable reason. Valid will: written, signed, two witnesses (holographic needs no witnesses). Application filed in probate court, usually the county where deceased lived. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/probate/probating-a-will)
- Muniment of title: option when estate has no debts other than real-estate liens or administration not needed; file court order and will with the county clerk of the county where the property sits. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/probate/probating-a-will; https://guides.sll.texas.gov/probate/informal-methods)
- Small estate affidavit (Estates Code ch. 205): no will; no administration applied for; assets $75,000 or less excluding homestead and exempt property; enough assets to pay debts; no real property except a homestead passing to spouse or minor children; all heirs agree; court must approve; homestead copy recorded with the county clerk. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/probate/informal-methods)
- County clerk manual adds: affidavit sworn by two disinterested witnesses; at least 30 days since death; decedent died intestate; no petition for personal representative pending or granted. (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)
- Affidavit of heirship is a sworn, court-unverified statement filed with the county clerk; no law forces third parties to accept it. Determination of heirship and order of no administration are other routes. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/probate/informal-methods)
- Inheritance tax: Senate Bill 752 (2015) repealed the Texas Inheritance Tax (Tax Code ch. 211) effective 09/01/2015. NOT OPENED: a Texas page stating there is no state estate tax. (https://star.comptroller.texas.gov/view/9609982H)
- Inherited real property: TODD needs affidavit of death and death certificate recorded; administered estate needs executor's or administrator's deed; muniment needs court order and will; otherwise affidavit of heirship. County cannot add heirs to the old deed. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/probate/property-deeds)

## 7. Land contract, quitclaim, transfer on death deed

- Executory contract (contract for deed, installment land contract, "lease to own"): Property Code ch. 5 subch. D governs contracts for conveyance of property used as the purchaser's residence (or a relative's); seller keeps title until paid in full. Seller must give an annual statement each January (liquidated damages language dates from 2005; check current amounts). (https://texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/opinion-files/opinion/2006/ga0418.pdf)
- Purchaser has a statutory right to cancel an executory contract; notice and cancellation time frames are in the Property Code. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/right-to-cancel)
- NOT OPENED: executory contract default and forfeiture rules (40 percent / 48 payments, cure periods, 30-day recording duty); AG and statute pages did not load.
- Quitclaim deed: listed among instruments recorded in deed records of the county clerk; recording needs signature and acknowledgment as in section 3. No other quitclaim text opened. (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)
- Transfer on death deed (Estates Code): passes real property without probate; beneficiaries may include charities; can be cancelled; must be recorded before the owner's death in the county where the property sits. Lady Bird (enhanced life estate) deed lets the owner sell or mortgage without beneficiary consent; no official form. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/wills-and-directives/transfer-property-after-death)

## 8. Squatters, adverse possession

- Adverse possession: "an actual and visible appropriation of real property, commenced and continued under a claim of right that is inconsistent with and is hostile to the claim of another person" (Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code 16.021(1)). Ten-year statute is 16.026; title passes if possessor stays the full period and meets statutory requirements. Claim is brought as trespass to try title. (https://txcourts.gov/media/1451686/190365.pdf)
- 3-year period (16.024): suit to recover land held in peaceable adverse possession under title or color of title. 5-year period (16.025): possessor cultivates, uses or enjoys the property, pays taxes, and claims under a duly registered deed. Source is a 1999 appeals opinion; confirm current text. (https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/14thCOA/Case/Opinions/111899/980047f.PDF)
- NOT OPENED: 25-year (16.027) period and current statutory text of 16.024 to 16.030.
- Removing squatters: Property Code ch. 24B lets an owner file a request for removal with the sheriff or constable, no court. Conditions: occupant stayed after being told to leave; property not open to public when entered; no pending suit between owner and occupant; occupant not family member or current/former tenant. Wrongful removal gives a lawsuit under 24B.005. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/eviction-process)
- Summary disposition (Prop. Code 24.0051606, introduced 2026) for squatters in a forcible entry and detainer suit: tenant response due in 4 days; if disputed, trial between 10 and 21 days after filing. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/eviction-process)

## 9. Tax lien and tax sale (tax foreclosure)

- A tax lien attaches Jan. 1 each year to secure that year's taxes; it lets courts foreclose and seize the property even if ownership changed; buyers cannot get clear title until delinquent taxes are paid. Owner on Jan. 1 is liable and can be sued even after a sale. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/pay; https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/calendars/deadlines.php)
- Collection ladder: delinquent notices, optional installment plan, then suit; court costs added to the bill. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/pay)
- Tax foreclosure sales are auctions typically by the constable or an agent hired by the taxing unit; no statewide list, check county clerk or county tax office sites. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/foreclosure/the-sale)
- Redemption of land sold at a tax sale (Tax Code 34.21), per Texas Supreme Court opinion: homestead, agricultural, or mineral interest property, two years from the date the purchaser's deed is filed, premium 25 percent in the first year or 50 percent in the second, plus bid, deed recording fee, taxes, penalties, interest and costs. Other property: 180th day after deed filed, premium not over 25 percent. Purchaser must itemize costs within 10 days of written request. (https://txcourts.gov/media/1444020/160874.pdf)
- Excess proceeds from a property tax foreclosure: former owner has two years from the sale to claim. (https://guides.sll.texas.gov/foreclosure/after-the-sale)
- State tax liens (Tax Code ch. 113): comptroller files notice with the county clerk, who records it in the state tax lien book. (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)

## 10. County organization

- Texas has 254 counties, from over 4 million people (Harris) to just over 100 (Loving). Counties levy property and sales taxes. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/transparency/local/counties.php)
- Appraisal district: chief appraiser appraises property each Jan. 1 and processes exemptions; a board of directors governs it; county tax assessor-collector sits as ex officio member in counties of 75,000 or more. ARB hears disputes. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/basics.php)
- County tax assessor-collector: many taxing units contract with the assessor-collector to collect all property taxes around Oct. 1 and remit them; appraisal districts do not levy tax. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/basics.php)
- County clerk: county recorder (deeds, deeds of trust, liens, notices of foreclosure sale), clerk of the commissioners court, and probate filings clerk (applications, small estate affidavits). (https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf)
