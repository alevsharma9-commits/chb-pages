# Florida shared fact sheet (read Nov 2026; official pages only)

Rule: every figure below is as printed on the cited page. Statute pages were the 2026 Florida Statutes unless noted.

## 1. Property tax calendar
- January 1 is the date of assessment; the property appraiser assesses all property at just value each year on January 1. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- March 1: deadline for property owners to file with the county property appraiser for exemptions or agricultural or other classifications. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- April 1: deadline for owners of tangible personal property to file Form DR-405 with the property appraiser. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Denial of exemption, classification, portability or tax deferral: petition the value adjustment board 30 days after the denial letter was mailed. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- August: the property appraiser mails the Notice of Proposed Property Taxes (TRIM notice, Form DR-474). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Value challenge: file a petition (one of the DR-486 forms) with the clerk of the court within 25 days of the Notice of Proposed Property Taxes. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- September/October: taxing authorities hold public hearings on tentative budgets and millage; October/November: hearings to adopt final budgets and millage rates. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- November: the tax collector sends tax bills (Form DR-528). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Early-payment discount as printed: "4 percent in November, 3 percent in December, 2 percent in January, and 1 percent in February." (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Unpaid taxes become delinquent on April 1 and tax certificates are sold on all unpaid items by June 1 (see s. 197.343). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Homestead tax deferral: file Form DR-570 with the tax collector by March 31 following the assessment year; deferral covers taxes above 5 percent of last year's household income (all taxes if household income under $10,000); a permanent resident 65 or older may defer the part above 3 percent of household income. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Installment prepayment: apply to the tax collector by May 1 of the assessment year (s. 197.222). Partial payments are at the tax collector's discretion before the delinquency date (s. 197.374). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Non-homestead assessed value increase is limited to 10 percent each year. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Growth in taxing-authority property tax revenue is capped at Florida per capita personal income growth plus new construction unless overridden by a super-majority, unanimous vote or referendum. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)

## 2. Homestead, Save Our Homes, portability, senior and veteran benefits
- Homestead exemption of up to $50,000: the first $25,000 applies to all property taxes including school taxes; the additional exemption of up to $25,000 applies to assessed value over $50,000 and only to non-school taxes (s. 196.031). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt113.pdf)
- The additional $25,000 is adjusted annually on January 1 for inflation by the CPI percentage change if positive; if CPI is negative the amount stays the same. Base year of the table is 2024. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt113.pdf)
- Apply with Form DR-501 to the county property appraiser by March 1 of the tax year; the owner must make the property the permanent residence by January 1. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt113.pdf)
- Homestead is lost if the unit is rented, is no longer the permanent home, or the owner is no longer a Florida permanent resident. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt113.pdf)
- Save Our Homes (SOH): after the first year of homestead and just-value assessment, the annual increase cannot exceed the lower of 3 percent or the CPI percentage change; assessed value never exceeds just value (s. 193.155). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt112.pdf)
- SOH cap chart printed by DOR (revised January 2026): 2026 CPI 2.7%, cap 2.7%; 2025 CPI 2.9%, cap 2.9%; 2024 CPI 3.4%, cap 3.0%. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/SaveOurHomes.pdf)
- Change of ownership (any sale, foreclosure, or transfer of legal or beneficial title) resets the property to just value the following January 1; exceptions include transfers between spouses and certain transfers upon death. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt112.pdf)
- Portability: establish the new homestead within three years of January 1 of the year the old homestead was abandoned (not three years after sale); file Form DR-501T with the DR-501 by March 1. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt112.pdf)
- Statute text: new-homestead assessed value reduced by the lesser of $500,000 or the just-minus-assessed difference of the prior homestead (s. 193.155(8)). Statute page notes ch. 2026-239 amendments "first apply to the 2027 ad valorem tax roll." (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0193/Sections/0193.155.html)
- Age 65 or older: county or city ordinance may allow an additional homestead exemption of up to $50,000 if household income is within the limitation (s. 196.075(2)); the income limit was $20,000 adjusted annually by CPI. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt110.pdf)
- Age 65 or older, 25-year permanent resident, just value under $250,000: ordinance may allow an exemption equal to the assessed value, subject to the income limit (s. 196.075(2)). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt110.pdf)
- Veterans 65 or older with permanent service-connected disability may receive a homestead tax discount equal to the VA disability percentage (s. 196.082); it carries to an unremarried surviving spouse. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt110.pdf)
- Disabled ex-servicemember (10 percent or more, honorably discharged, Florida resident): $5,000 reduction in assessed value, not limited to homestead (ss. 196.081, 196.091). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt109.pdf)
- Total and permanent service-connected disability (honorably discharged Florida resident): may be eligible for a total exemption from ad valorem taxes on homestead (s. 196.082); similar exemption for disabled veterans confined to wheelchairs. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt109.pdf)
- Deployed servicemembers: exemption equal to the percent of the prior year spent deployed on a designated operation (s. 196.173, Form DR-501M). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt109.pdf)
- A servicemember may rent out the homestead without abandoning the exemption (s. 196.061). (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt109.pdf)

## 3. Documentary stamp tax and recording fees
- Deed doc stamp tax: "on each $100 of the consideration therefor the tax shall be 70 cents" (s. 201.02(1)(a)); consideration includes money, discharge of an obligation, and any mortgage or encumbrance whether or not assumed. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0201/Sections/0201.02.html)
- DOR: all counties except Miami-Dade 70 cents per $100 or portion thereof; Miami-Dade 60 cents per $100 plus a 45 cents per $100 surtax, not due on a document transferring only a single-family dwelling (s. 201.031). (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
- Who pays: "All parties to the document are liable for the tax regardless of which party agrees to pay the tax. If a party is exempt, the tax must be paid by a non-exempt party." (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
- Tax is paid to the county clerk at recording; unrecorded taxable documents are paid to DOR. Deeds are taxable whether recorded or not. (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
- Taxable deeds listed by DOR include warranty, special warranty, quit claim, trustee's, life estate deeds, agreement/contract for deed, deed in lieu of foreclosure, and certificate of title. (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
- DOR: "There is no specific exemption for documents that transfer Florida real property for estate planning purposes." Personal representative's deed under a probated will is listed as not taxable. (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
- Spousal homestead deed where the only consideration is a mortgage, and divorce deeds for the marital home, are exempt (s. 201.02(7)). (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
- Mortgages and notes: 35 cents per $100 of the amount secured; tax on a note is capped at $2,450, no cap on mortgages. (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
- Foreclosure certificate of title: tax computed on the highest and best bid at the sale (s. 201.02(9)). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0201/Sections/0201.02.html)
- Clerk recording charges (s. 28.24(13)): $5.00 first page, $4.00 each additional page for instruments up to 14 by 8.5 inches; $1.00 per additional name beyond four; plus Public Records Modernization Trust Fund charge of $1.00 first page and $0.50 each additional page; plus an additional $4 per page technology charge. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0028/Sections/0028.24.html)
- Other clerk charges on the same page: tax deed application processing $60.00 (28.24(22)); plats first page $30.00, each additional $15.00 (28.24(12)). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0028/Sections/0028.24.html)

## 4. Judicial foreclosure
- Final judgment directs the clerk to sell at public sale not less than 20 days or more than 35 days after judgment (longer if plaintiff consents) (s. 45.031(1)). (https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/45.031)
- Notice of sale is published on a website per s. 50.0311 for at least 2 consecutive weeks, or in a newspaper once a week for 2 consecutive weeks. (https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/45.031)
- High bidder posts a deposit of 5 percent of the final bid; clerk files a certificate of sale; with no objections within 10 days, the clerk files the certificate of title and the sale stands confirmed. (https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/45.031)
- Final judgment must carry a surplus-funds notice; homestead owners are told to check with the clerk within 10 days after sale. Surplus claims must be filed before the clerk reports funds as unclaimed. (https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/45.031)
- Clerk may conduct the sale electronically. Bid amount is conclusively presumed sufficient consideration; objections to the bid are due within 10 days of the certificate of sale. (https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/45.031)
- Order to show cause path (s. 702.10): a lienholder may request it with a verified complaint; hearing no sooner than the later of 20 days after service of the order or 45 days after service of the complaint; the judgment is in rem only and a deficiency may still be allowed where permitted. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0702/Sections/0702.10.html)
- Attorney fees up to 3 percent of principal need no reasonableness hearing. The payment-or-vacate order does not apply to owner-occupied residences; a granted homestead exemption on the latest roll is a rebuttable presumption of owner-occupancy. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0702/Sections/0702.10.html)
- NOT OPENED: statute of limitations for foreclosure and the answer period after service.

## 5. Eviction
- Nonpayment: 3 days' written notice, excluding Saturday, Sunday and court-observed legal holidays, demanding payment or possession (s. 83.56(3)). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.56.html)
- Curable lease violation: 7 days to cure after delivery of notice; incurable violation (e.g. intentional property damage, subsequent or continued unreasonable disturbance): 7 days to vacate. Repeat within 12 months allows suit without new notice. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.56.html)
- Notice delivery: mailing, delivering a true copy, e-mail per s. 83.505, or leaving a copy at the residence if tenant is absent; notice requirements cannot be waived in the lease. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.56.html)
- Accepting partial rent after the 3-day notice: landlord must give a receipt with balance due, pay the partial rent into the court registry on filing, or post a new 3-day notice. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.56.html)
- Tenant raising any defense other than payment must pay accrued rent into the court registry (or file a motion to determine rent) within 5 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, after service, or waives defenses and the landlord gets an immediate default judgment (s. 83.60(2)). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.60.html)
- After judgment for the landlord the clerk issues a writ to the sheriff, who puts the landlord in possession after 24 hours' conspicuously posted notice; weekends and holidays do not stay the 24 hours (s. 83.62). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.62.html)
- Month-to-month tenancy ends with not less than 30 days' notice before the end of a monthly period; year-to-year 60 days; quarter-to-quarter 30 days; week-to-week 7 days (s. 83.57). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.57.html)

## 6. Probate, small estates, estate tax
- Summary administration is available if the non-exempt estate does not exceed $150,000 or the decedent has been dead more than 2 years (s. 735.201; page history shows 2026 amendment ch. 2026-57). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0735/Sections/0735.201.html)
- No administration is needed where only exempt personal property and limited nonexempt personal property (not above preferred funeral and last-60-day medical expenses) remain; court may authorize transfer on informal application (s. 735.301). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0735/Sections/0735.301.html)
- Creditor claims are barred unless filed by the later of 3 months after first publication of the notice to creditors or 30 days after service on a creditor required to be served (s. 733.702). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0733/Sections/0733.702.html)
- Estate tax: federal change eliminated Florida's estate tax for people who died after December 31, 2004; since July 1, 2023 personal representatives need not file Form DR-312 or DR-313. (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/pages/estate_tax.aspx)
- NOT OPENED: formal administration steps (petition, letters of administration, two-year outer claim bar s. 733.710).

## 7. Land contract, quitclaim, transfers, lady bird deed
- Conveyance of an interest in land (freehold or term over 1 year) must be in writing signed in the presence of two subscribing witnesses; no seal needed; witnessing may be by audio-video technology (s. 689.01). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0689/Sections/0689.01.html)
- Quit claim deeds and agreements/contracts for deed are listed by DOR as documents subject to doc stamp tax. (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
- A quitclaim or other transfer of title is a "change of ownership" for SOH unless an exception applies (e.g. same person remains entitled, spouse transfers, correcting an error). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0193/Sections/0193.155.html)
- Lady bird (enhanced life estate) deed: DOR advisement TAA 20B4-004 (Oct 16, 2020) says it "does not transfer any present beneficial interests," so it is "not subject to documentary stamp tax regardless of any consideration," on the facts presented (life tenants retained full powers). (https://floridarevenue.com/TaxLaw/Documents/20B4-004.pdf)
- A recorded contract for deed vendee is among those notified before a tax deed sale (s. 197.502(4)(d)). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0197/Sections/0197.502.html)
- NOT OPENED: a Florida land contract (installment sale) statute beyond doc stamp tax; recording-formality statute s. 695.26.

## 8. Squatters and adverse possession
- Adverse possession (s. 95.18): 7 years actual continued possession under a claim of title not founded on a written instrument; possessor must pay all outstanding taxes within 1 year of entering, file a return with the property appraiser within 30 days after that, and keep paying taxes. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0095/Sections/0095.18.html)
- Possession means protected by substantial enclosure or cultivated, maintained or improved in a usual manner. The property appraiser mails the return to the record owner and notes the claim on the tax roll. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0095/Sections/0095.18.html)
- Occupying a residential structure solely by adverse possession claim before filing the return is trespass (s. 810.08); offering it for lease is theft (s. 812.014). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0095/Sections/0095.18.html)
- Sheriff removal (s. 82.036): owner or agent submits a verified Complaint to Remove Persons Unlawfully Occupying Residential Real Property; conditions include no tenancy, not family, property not open to the public, owner directed them to leave, and no pending litigation. Sheriff serves a notice to immediately vacate and puts the owner in possession. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0082/Sections/0082.036.html)
- False statements or wrongful use expose the owner to damages including statutory damages equal to triple the fair market rent, costs and attorney fees. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0082/Sections/0082.036.html)
- NOT OPENED: adverse possession under color of title (s. 95.16).

## 9. Tax certificates and tax deeds
- Sale: tax collector awards each certificate to the bidder demanding the lowest interest rate, in quarter-percent increments; unsold certificates are struck to the county at the maximum rate (s. 197.432(6)). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=0100-0199/0197/Sections/0197.432.html)
- Homestead property with under $250 delinquent must be issued to the county, not sold at auction; tax collector commission is 5 percent. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=0100-0199/0197/Sections/0197.432.html)
- Certificate holder may not contact the owner to demand payment until 2 years after April 1 of the year of issuance. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=0100-0199/0197/Sections/0197.432.html)
- Tax deed application: holder may file after 2 years have elapsed since April 1 of the year of issuance; application fee of $75. Tax collector lists persons to be noticed (titleholder, lienholders, mortgagees, contract-for-deed vendees, assessed party). (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0197/Sections/0197.502.html)
- Opening bid on homestead property includes one-half of the latest assessed value in addition to the amounts due on non-homestead property. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0197/Sections/0197.502.html)
- No bidders: land goes on the "lands available for taxes" list; the county has 90 days to buy for the opening bid; 3 years after the offer the land escheats to the county. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0197/Sections/0197.502.html)
- Sale (s. 197.542): clerk holds the public auction; interest on the certificate holder's bid accrues at 1.5 percent per month; high bidder posts a nonrefundable deposit of 5 percent of the bid or $200, whichever is greater, with full payment, doc stamps and recording fees due within 24 hours (excluding weekends and holidays). Electronic sales allowed. (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0197/Sections/0197.542.html)
- NOT OPENED: certificate redemption mechanics and maximum certificate interest rate (ss. 197.172, 197.472).

## 10. County organization
- Statutes divide Florida into 67 counties; the Constitution recognizes charter and non-charter counties. (https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2023/1373/Analyses/h1373c.JDC.PDF)
- Each county elects a sheriff, tax collector, property appraiser, supervisor of elections and clerk of the circuit court to four-year terms. (https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2023/1373/Analyses/h1373c.JDC.PDF)
- Property appraiser: assesses at just value each January 1, mails the TRIM notice, and decides exemption eligibility. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Tax collector: sends tax bills, collects payment, sells tax certificates and takes tax deferral and installment applications. (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers.aspx)
- Clerk of the circuit court: records documents and collects doc stamp tax at recording, receives VAB petitions, runs foreclosure and tax deed sales. (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
