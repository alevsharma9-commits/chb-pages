# North Carolina shared fact sheet

Read from official pages only (ncleg.gov statutes, ncdor.gov, nccourts.gov). Figures quoted as the page prints them. Every bullet ends with its source.

## 1. Property tax calendar

- Listing period: begins the first business day of January and ends January 31. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-307.html)
- Listing extensions: county commissioners may extend up to 30 additional days in nonrevaluation years and up to 60 additional days in years of octennial appraisal. Individual extensions for good cause on written request filed by the end of the regular listing period; may not extend beyond April 15 (electronic personal property listing: June 1). (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-307.html)
- Due date: taxes are due and payable September 1 of the fiscal year for which levied. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-360.html)
- Payable at par or face amount if paid before January 6 following the due date; taxes paid on or after January 6 are subject to interest. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-360.html)
- Interest: January 6 to February 1 = two percent (2%); from February 1 until paid = three-fourths of one percent (3/4%) a month or fraction thereof. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-360.html)
- Prepayment discounts: a county or municipality may adopt a discount schedule (resolution by May 1 preceding the due date, approved by Department of Revenue). Mailed payments are deemed received on the USPS postmark date. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-360.html)
- County board of equalization and review: made up of the board of county commissioners unless a special board is created. First meeting not earlier than the first Monday in April and not later than the first Monday in May. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-322.html)
- Board duration: in nonrevaluation years it completes its duties on or before the third Monday following its first meeting (unless longer is necessary) and may not sit later than July 1 except for certain requests; in a revaluation year it completes its duties on or before December 1. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-322.html)
- Taxpayer appeal to the county board: request a hearing in writing or by personal appearance before the board adjourns; if the board's notice of a decision was mailed fewer than 15 days before adjournment, the request may be made within 15 days after the notice was mailed. Board mails its decision no later than 30 days after adjournment. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-322.html)
- Next level: notice of appeal to the Property Tax Commission is filed within 30 days after the date the board mailed notice of its decision to the property owner; notice must be in writing, state the grounds, and a copy goes to the county assessor. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-290.html)
- Revaluation cycle: each county must reappraise all real property every eighth year (octennial cycle) as of January 1 of the scheduled year. Mandatory advancement for a county of 75,000 or more population when its sales assessment ratio is less than .85 or greater than 1.15 (reappraisal effective by January 1 of the earlier of the third year after the notice or the eighth year after the last reappraisal). Counties may advance voluntarily by resolution and may set a more frequent cycle. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-286.html)

## 2. Homestead exclusion and relief programs

- Elderly or disabled exclusion (G.S. 105-277.1): excluded amount is the greater of $25,000 or fifty percent (50%) of the appraised value of the permanent residence. Owner must as of January 1 preceding the taxable year be at least 65 or totally and permanently disabled, have prior-year income not over the income eligibility limit, and be a North Carolina resident. May not receive other property tax relief. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-277.1.html)
- Income limit: $38,800 for the 2026 tax year (was $37,900 for 2025); adjusted yearly by the Social Security cost-of-living percentage. (https://ncdor.gov/2026-av-9-securedpdf/open)
- Definitions: "income" is all moneys received from every source other than gifts or inheritances from a spouse, lineal ancestor or lineal descendant (both spouses counted if residing together). Permanent residence = dwelling, dwelling site not to exceed one acre, related improvements; may be a single-family home, a unit in a multi-family complex, or a manufactured home. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-277.1.html)
- Disabled applicants need a physician's certificate (NC-licensed) or a governmental agency certificate; a married couple gets the full exclusion if only one spouse qualifies. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-277.1.html)
- Disabled veteran exclusion (G.S. 105-277.1C): the first $45,000 of appraised value is excluded. Veteran with honorable or under-honorable-conditions separation who received 38 U.S.C. 2101 benefits or has VA certification of service-connected, permanent and total disability; unmarried surviving spouse also qualifies. Owner must be a North Carolina resident. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-277.1C.html)
- NCDOR form text: "There is no age or income limitation for this program." Applicants file Form NCDVA-9 (completed first by a Veterans Service Officer) with the county assessor. (https://ncdor.gov/2026-av-9-securedpdf/open)
- Circuit breaker (G.S. 105-277.1B): taxes limited to 4.0% of income if income is up to the income eligibility limit and 5.0% if income is over the limit up to 150% of it. Owner must be 65 or totally and permanently disabled, NC resident, and have owned and occupied the property as a permanent residence at least five years. Excess tax is deferred and is a lien; deferred taxes for the preceding three fiscal years become due on a disqualifying event (transfer, death of owner, ceasing to use as permanent residence). (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-277.1B.html)
- 2026 circuit breaker thresholds as printed by NCDOR: $38,800 limit and $58,200 (150%); a new application must be filed every year. (https://ncdor.gov/2026-av-9-securedpdf/open)
- Application: file on Form AV-9 with the county tax assessor (not the Department of Revenue); should be filed during the listing period but must be accepted through June 1 preceding the tax year. Each owner may receive benefit from only one of the three programs. (https://ncdor.gov/2026-av-9-securedpdf/open)

## 3. Excise tax on deeds and recording

- Excise tax: levied on each instrument conveying an interest in real property; rate is "one dollar ($1.00) on each five hundred dollars ($500.00) or fractional part thereof of the consideration or value of the interest conveyed." (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-228.30.html)
- Who pays: "The transferor must pay the tax to the register of deeds of the county in which the real estate is located before recording the instrument of conveyance." For a parcel in two or more counties, the county holding the greater value. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-228.30.html)
- Distribution: one-half to the county general fund, one-half (less refunds and allowance) remitted monthly to the Department of Revenue; county may retain 2% of the State share. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-228.30.html)
- Recording effect: no conveyance, contract to convey, option, lease over three years, right of first refusal or right of first offer is valid against lien creditors or purchasers for valuable consideration except from the time of registration in the county where the land lies. Priority follows order of registration by time. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_47/GS_47-18.html)
- Uniform statewide recording fees (G.S. 161-10): instruments in general $26.00 for the first 15 pages plus $4.00 for each additional page; deeds of trust and mortgages $64.00 for the first 35 pages plus $4.00 for each additional page. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_161/GS_161-10.html)
- Other fees on that page: no fee for recording satisfactions or cancellations of deeds of trust or mortgages; plats $21.00 per sheet; certified copy $5.00 first page plus $2.00 each additional page; nonstandard document add-on $25.00; additional instruments in one document $10.00 each. Fees are "complete and exclusive" and collected before filing. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_161/GS_161-10.html)

## 4. Foreclosure

- Type: power-of-sale foreclosure under a mortgage or deed of trust; the mortgagee or trustee files a notice of hearing with the clerk of superior court, and the hearing is before the clerk in the county where the land lies. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_45/GS_45-21.16.html)
- Notice: notice of hearing served not less than 10 days before the hearing; posting on the property by the sheriff not less than 20 days before where publication would be authorized or service cannot be made. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_45/GS_45-21.16.html)
- Clerk must find: valid debt held by the foreclosing party, default, right to foreclose under the instrument, and notice to those entitled; for home loans also that the pre-foreclosure notice was given. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_45/GS_45-21.16.html)
- Appeal of the clerk's finding: to the district or superior court judge within 10 days; owner-occupant appeal bond is 1% of the principal balance unless reduced. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_45/GS_45-21.16.html)
- Notice of sale for residential property with fewer than 15 rental units must state that an order for possession may issue; tenants under rental agreements entered or renewed on or after October 1, 2007 may terminate on at least 10 but no more than 90 days' notice after the sale date. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_45/GS_45-21.16A.html)
- Upset bid: must exceed the reported sale price or last upset bid by at least 5% with a minimum increase of $750.00; deposit of at least 5% of the upset bid but not less than $750.00; filed by close of business on the tenth day after the report of sale or last notice of upset bid. Each upset bid is followed by a new 10-day period; rights become fixed when no upset bid is filed in time. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_45/GS_45-21.27.html)

## 5. Eviction

- Notice to quit by tenancy type: year to year, one month or more before the end of the current year; month to month, seven days; week to week, two days; manufactured home space rental, at least 60 days before the end of the current rental period. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_42/GS_42-14.html)
- Nonpayment: lease term is forfeited on failure to pay rent within 10 days after demand for all past-due rent, and the lessor may then enter and dispossess the tenant. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_42/GS_42-3.html)
- Summary ejectment grounds (G.S. 42-26): holding over after term expires, breach of a lease condition ending the estate, or desertion while in arrears. Water, sewer or electric charge arrears cannot be the basis for lease termination. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_42/GS_42-26.html)
- Court process: eviction is filed in small claims before a magistrate; court dates are set within 30 days of filing and "usually within 10 to 15 days for eviction cases." Continuance is mandatory if the defendant was served fewer than 2 days before the court date in eviction cases. (https://nccourts.gov/help-topics/lawsuits-and-small-claims/small-claims)
- Small claims filing fee printed as $96; sheriff service fee printed as $30; no jury trial in small claims. (https://nccourts.gov/help-topics/lawsuits-and-small-claims/small-claims)
- Appeal: notice of appeal to district court orally in court or in writing within 10 days after the magistrate's decision; appeal costs must be paid and a bond may be required to stop enforcement. (https://nccourts.gov/help-topics/lawsuits-and-small-claims/small-claims)

## 6. Probate, small estates, estate tax

- Small estate by affidavit, intestate: personal property less liens not exceeding $20,000, any time after 30 days from the date of death, by an heir, creditor or public administrator; no petition for a personal representative may be pending or granted. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_28A/GS_28A-25-1.html)
- Surviving spouse who is the sole heir: limit may exceed $20,000 but not exceed $30,000 after reduction for any spousal allowance. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_28A/GS_28A-25-1.html)
- Affidavit is filed with the clerk of superior court of the county where the decedent was domiciled; the statute lists a description of each tract of real property the decedent owned in the affidavit. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_28A/GS_28A-25-1.html)
- Estate tax: G.S. 105-32.1 through 105-32.8 "Repealed by Session Laws 2013-316, s.7(a), effective January 1, 2013, and applicable to the estates of decedents dying on or after that date." (https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByArticle/Chapter_105/Article_1A.html)
- NOT OPENED: full probate procedure for estates above the small estate limit (letters testamentary, creditor claim period).

## 7. Land contracts, quitclaim, transfer on death

- Contract for deed (Chapter 47H) covers an agreement with five or more payments exclusive of down payment where the seller retains title, for 1 to 4 family principal dwellings or manufactured homes with price of $5,000 or more. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByChapter/Chapter_47H.html)
- Must be written, signed and acknowledged; purchaser may cancel until midnight of the third business day after execution or delivery; seller must record the contract or a "Memorandum of a Contract for Deed" within five business days. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByChapter/Chapter_47H.html)
- Forfeiture requires a written notice of default and intent to forfeit giving a cure date not less than 30 days after service; reinstated on timely cure; equity of redemption ends only by recorded mutual termination or court order. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByChapter/Chapter_47H.html)
- Other 47H terms: seller must hold title (limited exceptions for liens); late fee capped at 4% and only on payments more than 15 days past due; annual statement of account. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByChapter/Chapter_47H.html)
- NOT OPENED: quitclaim deed rules.
- NOT OPENED: transfer on death deed or beneficiary deed status.

## 8. Squatters and adverse possession

- Twenty years adverse possession: possession "under known and visible lines and boundaries adversely to all other persons for 20 years" bars an action to recover the property and gives title in fee against persons not under disability. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_1/GS_1-40.html)
- NOT OPENED: squatter removal and unlawful occupancy statute; shorter color-of-title period (G.S. 1-38).

## 9. Tax lien and tax sale

- Lien attaches to the parcel on the date as of which property is to be listed under G.S. 105-285; penalties, interest and costs are added and attach at the same time. A lien for personal property taxes attaches to all real property of the taxpayer in the taxing unit. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-355.html)
- Enforcement by foreclosure action "in the nature of an action to foreclose a mortgage" filed in the county where the property lies; owner of record, spouse and other lienholders are served. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-374.html)
- Sale: public auction to the highest bidder at the courthouse door; commissioner may require a deposit up to 20% of the bid; sale is in fee simple free of most liens. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-374.html)
- Commissioner reports the sale within three days; exceptions or an increased bid may be filed within 10 days after the report; confirmation can be sought after the 10 days; commissioner fee up to five percent (5%) of purchase price. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-374.html)
- Redemption: before confirmation the person redeeming pays all taxes due on the property plus penalties, interest and costs; remaining sale proceeds after taxes go to the court for the persons entitled. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-374.html)
- NOT OPENED: G.S. 105-375 (in rem foreclosure) and G.S. 105-361 (other collection remedies).

## 10. County organization

- Register of deeds: elected biennially by the qualified voters of each county. (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_161/GS_161-1.html)
- Register of deeds records instruments of conveyance, collects the uniform fees and collects the deed excise tax before recording (see sections 3 above). (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_161/GS_161-10.html)
- County boards of commissioners act as boards of equalization and review and set listing extensions (see section 1). (https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-322.html)
